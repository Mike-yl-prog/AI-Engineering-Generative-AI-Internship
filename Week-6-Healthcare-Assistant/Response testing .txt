Response Testing
The prototype was tested using realistic user questions across five categories: normal supported questions, ambiguous questions, unsupported requests, requests requiring escalation, and questions where information is unavailable. The purpose was to assess whether the assistant provides accurate Knowledge Base responses, avoids hallucinating information, stays within its administrative scope, and escalates appropriately.

Important Finding: Payment Testing
The payment section revealed an important issue during earlier testing. At one point, the assistant generated unsupported payment methods such as EFTPOS and cheques, and also generated unsupported claims about payment plans, financing and discounts.

These responses did not match the approved Knowledge Base.

This demonstrates why payment and billing questions require stronger response testing. The testing also helped identify the need to reinforce the instruction:

If information is not contained in the approved Knowledge Base, the assistant must not guess or invent an answer. It should direct the user to clinic reception.

After reinforcing this requirement, subsequent tests for cryptocurrency, discounts, insurance, instalments and cancellation fees produced safer responses.

Overall Response Testing Result
The response testing demonstrated that the prototype can successfully handle the majority of approved administrative questions while maintaining important safety boundaries.

Overall findings:

Normal supported questions: Good performance; information was retrieved accurately.
Ambiguous questions: Generally handled appropriately without making unsupported assumptions.
Unsupported requests: Appropriate refusal/escalation was observed.
Medical/safety requests: The assistant correctly avoided diagnosis, treatment and medication advice.
Emergency requests: The assistant directed users toward immediate appropriate emergency help.
Unavailable information: Generally handled correctly, although payment/billing testing initially exposed hallucination risks.
Booking actions: The assistant correctly stated that it cannot access or control a live booking system.
Key improvement: Strengthen Knowledge Base grounding, particularly for payment, insurance, pricing, discounts and other areas where the Knowledge Base contains limited information.
Conclusion: The testing shows that the HealthConnect prototype is suitable as an administrative and informational assistant prototype, provided that Knowledge Base grounding and refusal/escalation rules continue to be enforced and tested.
