Hallucination risks
The main hallucination risk is that the assistant can produce confident, plausible-sounding recruitment information without proving that the information comes from TETRAYL's authoritative data. For example, the CTO response gives a generic role description, but it is unclear whether those requirements actually belong to TETRAYL.

Mitigation: The assistant should use retrieval from verified company sources for job descriptions, policies, candidate records, and application status. If the required information is unavailable, it should explicitly state that it cannot verify it.

Bias considerations
Because this assistant operates in recruitment, biased outputs could have significant consequences. Candidate comparisons, resume-gap questions, and recommendations should be based only on job-relevant qualifications and objective criteria.

Mitigation: Use standardized evaluation criteria, avoid protected characteristics and irrelevant personal information, regularly test outputs for disparate treatment, and ensure that AI recommendations are reviewed by humans.

Privacy concerns
Candidate information is highly sensitive. The test in which a user requested a list of candidate salaries demonstrated a positive privacy behavior: the assistant did not disclose the requested information.

Mitigation: Implement role-based access control, authentication, data minimization, encryption, secure storage, and audit logs. The assistant should verify authorization before accessing or revealing candidate information.

Security considerations
The assistant successfully resisted the direct prompt-injection attempt to reveal its system prompt. This indicates a useful baseline security control.

Mitigation: Continue adversarial testing against prompt injection, indirect instructions, attempts to extract confidential candidate information, and attempts to bypass authorization. Sensitive information should be protected at the system/data-access layer, not only through the model's instructions.

Ethical limitations
The assistant should not be treated as the final decision-maker in recruitment. AI-generated candidate rankings or recommendations can contain errors or unintended bias.

Mitigation: Use the assistant as a support tool for tasks such as summarization, question generation, and communication drafting. Final hiring, rejection, compensation, and other employment decisions should remain under human control.

Human oversight recommendations
Human review should be mandatory for:

Candidate selection and rejection
Candidate comparisons and rankings
Interview evaluation
Salary and compensation decisions
Exceptions to recruitment policies
Disputed or sensitive candidate cases
Any decision that could materially affect a candidate's employment opportunity
Overall, the assistant demonstrates good initial mitigation of prompt injection and confidential-data requests, but stronger grounding, access controls, bias monitoring, and mandatory human oversight are needed before using it for high-impact recruitment decisions.
